# Vision

## Our Vision

We envision a future where frontend performance engineering is no longer a specialized skill reserved for experts.

Instead, every developer—regardless of experience, framework, company size, or IDE—receives immediate, trustworthy, and actionable performance guidance while writing code.

Frontend Performance Lab becomes the engineering intelligence layer that continuously helps developers build software that is fast, scalable, and maintainable.

---

## What We Are Building

FPL is not simply another linting tool.

It is not another Lighthouse report.

It is not another AI chatbot.

FPL is building an entirely new category:

**AI-Assisted Deterministic Performance Engineering.**

Every recommendation begins with deterministic analysis.

Artificial Intelligence exists to explain, prioritize, educate, and accelerate—not to invent problems.

---

## Ten-Year Vision

A frontend developer should be able to open any repository, in any supported IDE, and immediately receive expert-level performance guidance without configuration, external services, or manual investigation.

Performance engineering should become as natural as syntax highlighting.

---

## Platform Vision

FPL will become a platform rather than a single product.

The platform will consist of:

- A deterministic analysis engine
- A diagnostics engine
- A reporting engine
- A plugin ecosystem
- An MCP server
- IDE integrations
- A public SDK
- A marketplace for performance plugins

Each layer builds upon the previous one while remaining independently testable and maintainable.

---

## Community Vision

We believe the best engineering knowledge should be shared.

Frontend Performance Lab will foster an open ecosystem where contributors can build analyzers, diagnostics, educational recipes, reports, plugins, and integrations without needing to modify the core engine.

The community should extend FPL—not fork it.

---

## Product Vision

Developers should eventually think of FPL the same way they think about Git.

Not because it is mandatory.

Because it becomes difficult to imagine professional software development without it.

---

## Success

We succeed when developers trust FPL enough to act on its recommendations without questioning whether they are real.

Trust—not popularity—is our primary metric.
